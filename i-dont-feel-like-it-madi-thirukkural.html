“I Don’t Feel Like It Now…” — Madi: When Comfort Begins to Choose for Us

  • 10th October 2026

“Neduneer maravi madi thuyil naangum
Keduneerar kaamak kalan.”

— Thirukkural 605

In this journey through Thiruvalluvar’s four quiet obstacles, we have already encountered two familiar voices.

The first said:
“I’ll do it later…”

That was Neduneer — postponement.

The second said:
“I’ll remember…”

That led us to Maravi — forgetfulness.

Now Arun’s story brings us to a different situation.

He is no longer postponing something because there is plenty of time. He has not forgotten what needs to be done. In fact, the application is open right in front of him.

He remembers.
He knows.
He wants the opportunity.

And yet...
he does not act.

Why?

Arun Opens the Application

Several days have passed since Arun first discovered the training programme. The deadline is approaching. Finally, the application comes back to his attention.

He opens the form. There are a few questions that require thoughtful answers. He will have to sit down, think and write.

The moment he sees the work involved, something inside him resists.

He thinks:
“This seems like quite a bit of work…”

And then:
“Not today. I’ll do it tomorrow when I’m fresh.”

Notice what has happened.

The opportunity still matters to him. The task is clear. The application is open. Nothing outside him is preventing him from continuing.

But effort is required. And immediate comfort feels easier.

The third ship has arrived.

Its voice is:
“I don’t feel like doing it now…”

This is Madi — மடி.

Madi Is Not Rest

Before exploring Madi, we need to make an important distinction.

Rest is not laziness.

Someone who has worked hard may genuinely need to rest. A student who has been studying intensely may need a break. A person recovering physically or mentally may need more rest than usual.

The body and mind require recovery. There is nothing wrong with that. In fact, proper rest supports meaningful action.

The problem is different.

It begins when we know that something needs to be done, we are reasonably capable of doing it, but we repeatedly avoid the necessary effort because something more comfortable is available.

Rest prepares us to return to action.
Madi keeps us away from the action that matters.

The two may sometimes look similar from the outside. The difference becomes clearer when we look honestly at what is happening within us.

When Immediate Comfort Competes with What Matters

Imagine a simple evening. You have decided to go for a walk. Your shoes are ready. But the sofa is comfortable.

You intended to read. The book is beside you. But the phone is already in your hand.

There is an important conversation you need to have. But it may be uncomfortable. So:
“We’ll talk later.”

You intended to meditate. But instead of sitting down to meditate, you watch another video about meditation.

You know what needs to be done. Yet another option offers less effort and more immediate comfort.

This reveals something important about our choices. We do not always choose between “good” and “bad.” Many of our daily choices are much subtler.

They may be between:
What feels comfortable now
and
what actually matters to us.

The Problem Is Not One Comfortable Choice

Choosing comfort once is not the problem. Watching a movie is not Madi. Sitting on the sofa is not Madi. Using a phone is not Madi. Taking a break is not Madi.

Entertainment is not the enemy.

The question is not:
“Am I enjoying something?”

The more useful question is:
“What am I choosing this instead of?”

Am I scrolling after completing what matters? Or am I scrolling to avoid beginning it?

Am I resting because I genuinely need recovery? Or because the next action feels uncomfortable?

Am I watching something because I consciously chose recreation? Or because I do not want to face what is waiting for me?

The activity may look identical from the outside. The inner movement can be very different.

When we notice Madi, we do not need to condemn ourselves. We can pause, recognise what we are avoiding, and choose one small step toward what matters. Sometimes that step is to begin; sometimes it is to rest with the intention of returning.
